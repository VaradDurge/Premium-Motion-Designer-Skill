# Creative system

## Hierarchy

1. Story clarity
2. Product truth
3. The still (composition and type)
4. Motion
5. Sound
6. Finish

Effects do not repair a weak still or a vague thesis. Finish is last: shadow, grain, and light may support a frame that already works.

## System, decided once

Write these down and stop adding ingredients:

- **Ground:** the product's real canvas. Paper, ink, or a flat field is for a showreel, or for a product that already looks like that. Do not restyle a product into that look.
- **Accent:** the product's accent, used for the active control, the proof, and one word. If the brand is a gradient or several colors, use those. Do not invent a second palette.
- **Type:** the product's real face if it is good; otherwise one pairing, with a size for the claim and a size for the label
- **Motif:** one shape, path, or object that can recur
- **Continuity object:** what is allowed to survive a cut
- **Easing family:** the curves in [motion-craft.md](motion-craft.md)
- **Grid:** the product's own layout, cropped, for a launch or a demo. Poster, stage, or chapter is for a showreel.

A second accent is allowed when it has a meaning (danger, success) that the product already uses. A third accent is decoration.

## Premium, in practice

- One focal point
- Holds on the product state. Empty type frames belong in a showreel, not in the middle of a launch.
- Pacing with holds
- A palette you could name in one breath
- Type that reads at playback size
- A camera that arrives
- The same spatial logic from shot to shot
- UI large enough to be the product, not a sticker
- One surprise, prepared by the shots before it

## Cheap, in practice

- Everything moves at once
- The same centered stack every beat
- Glow as a material
- Type that needs a pause button
- A different gradient per scene
- Glass cards as the idea
- Cuts that share nothing
- Finish hiding a weak layout
- Invented UI or invented numbers
- More ideas than the runtime can hold

## Type

- Brand fonts when they are good on screen
- Hierarchy chosen before any letter moves
- Line breaks and tracking are design, not defaults
- Optical alignment over mathematical alignment when a lockup looks off
- No fake editorial styling (giant italics, random serifs) unless the brand is that

## Camera and depth

Consistent perspective. Dolly, push, or parallax only when the move reveals something. Macro for proof, wide for orientation. 3D when the idea has volume. A slow orbit of a random object is not a camera language.

## Finish

Allowed, small: contact shadow, a little grain, a light that explains a surface.

Spare: chromatic aberration, flare, scanlines, heavy grain, bloom on every edge. Stacking them is how weak compositions ask to be taken seriously.
