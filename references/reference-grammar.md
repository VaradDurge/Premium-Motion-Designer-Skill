# Reference grammar

These notes come from watching product launches and short motion films, plus the argument those films started about authorship.

Use the principles. Do not reproduce their layouts, copy, products, characters, or end cards.

If a reference cannot be opened, do not invent what was in it. Ask for the file or keep going from references you actually watched.

## Authorship

A film that retraces someone else's shots, with the ending swapped to your logo or to the model that rendered it, is not direction. It is a copy.

Take pacing, contrast, type behavior, and how little a frame needs. Leave their story, their product, their sentences, and their composition.

The last frame belongs to the product being advertised. Do not end on the model, the editor, or the framework unless that tool is the product.

## Two legal structures

**Product film.** One belief, proven in order: a real action, the problem getting specific, the mechanism made visible, one proof, a sentence, the brand. Readable product UI is on screen for at least half the runtime. The product appears before the logo. One state change is caused by a cursor or by data. A type reel with a short UI insert is not a launch. Numbers are real and singular. The brand arrives late and smaller than the claim.

**Showreel.** A point of view about motion, not a fake product. One motif transforms (a dot, a crop, a window, a grid). Each cut is a finished poster. Color fields replace transitions. The lockup is the ending. A showreel that also pretends to demo a product, with invented charts, is neither.

Do not build a product film with showreel logic (a tour of effects), and do not build a showreel by faking a startup inside it.

"Go all out" on a showreel still needs one system. A minute that visits kinetic type, bauhaus patterns, terrain, glitch, glass, and a fake dashboard is a catalog. Catalogs feel generated even when each shot is impressive.

## Principles worth keeping

### One idea owns the frame

The frames people remember are almost empty: a control and a cursor, a word cropped by the edge, a single dot on a dark field, one number, one sentence with one word in the accent. If two ideas are on screen, one of them is early or late.

### A still is the design

The settled frame works as a poster. Motion is how you get there and how you leave. If you need the move to understand the layout, the layout is not done.

### Continuity is an object, not a crossfade

One object holds its screen position across a hard cut. A window, a word, or a cursor can be that object. That is what makes cuts feel like one piece.

### Color changes are cuts

Paper, ink, and one saturated field. The field change is the transition. Fog, glow, and a different gradient per scene are how films avoid deciding.

### Type is physical

It gets cropped, masked, tracked into a lockup, or set in a poster grid beside one object. It is not a caption floating in the middle. Repeated words as a texture are a specific idea — use them once, if the repetition is the point.

### The cursor is a character

It enters with a purpose, hesitates, acts, and causes the next state. Product films that open on a real control being used feel like software. Product films that open on a title feel like templates.

### Proof is singular

One percentage, then another, in the same spot. One latency, then the improved latency, same layout. A dashboard of six charts proves nothing in two seconds.

### The claim is a sentence

A claim is one sentence. Four feature badges are a webpage. Reveal the sentence simply. At 30fps, hold it for 45–90 frames.

### A grid makes loudness work

Huge type beside one object, same margins every time, can be loud and still feel designed. The same type in the center of a new background every second feels automated. Chrome, glass, and 3D earn their place only inside a grid. In the middle of a gradient, they are stickers.

### A world is a material

Illustrated or essay films work when the characters, the windows, the errors, and the type all feel made of the same stuff. Do not paste a flat UI onto an unrelated world, and do not cartoon a serious product unless that is the brand.

### Process footage is not a visual style

A screen recording of a timeline, a wall of thumbnails, or selection handles can explain how a piece was made. That chrome is not a look to borrow for a launch film, unless the product is the design tool and the chrome is the subject.

### Openings and endings

- Something readable immediately: a word, a typed action, a UI state.
- The first cut can wait until that idea has registered.
- The end is a lockup with a line of meaning, held, not a logo fading in because the music ended.
- The lockup is a beat, not a third of the film. If the logo is still on screen after the claim has been read, cut.

### Sentence, then the proof

Product films that stay clear alternate a short line with the UI that makes the line true. The sentence and the screenshot should not fight in the same frame unless one is the label of the other.

### The result has to leave the screen

A strong launch shows the outcome, not only the interface: the thing the user now has, the message that went out, the number that changed. The UI is the mechanism. The last proof is the consequence.

### What looks like work, and is not a film

- A screen recording of a model typing code, or of a timeline filling itself, is a process clip. It can explain how something was made. It is not a product film.
- A 3D model spinning in a software viewport, settings panel still open, is a turntable. It becomes a film only when the viewport is gone and the object is staged.
- An essay can run long if every scene is the same material (one paper, one kind of card, one accent) and each scene adds one claim. A new visual language per chapter is a slideshow.

## What the weak version of each idea looks like

| Strong idea | Weak substitute |
|---|---|
| One accent on paper or ink | A new gradient every scene |
| Hard cut or a shared object | Blur in, blur out |
| One readable UI state | A full product screenshot, small |
| One real number | A fake analytics card |
| Poster grid | Centered stack of cards |
| Cursor causes a result | Cursor wanders decoratively |
| Brand last, with a sentence | Model logo as the ending |
| Holds | Constant motion |
| Two or three techniques | Every technique in the file |

## How to write borrow / will-not-copy

After watching references, write three lines of grammar and three refusals.

Good borrow: "Hard cuts between flat fields. One object survives. Type is cropped by the frame, then settles. Proof is a single number."

Bad borrow: "Use their orange, their sentence, their food app, their end card."

Good refusal: "I will not retell their product story. I will not end on the renderer. I will not paste their layout onto this brand."
